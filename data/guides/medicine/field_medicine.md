# Field Medicine Guide — VaultMind

> **WARNING**: This guide is a reference for extreme survival scenarios where
> no professional medical care is available. Always seek qualified medical
> help when possible. Incorrect application can cause serious harm or death.

## Wound Care

### Situation
Open wounds are the #1 infection risk in grid-down scenarios. Without
antibiotics, even minor cuts can become fatal sepsis within days.

### Cleaning a Wound
1. Wash your hands thoroughly with soap (or ash-water if no soap)
2. Irrigate the wound with clean water under pressure (squeeze bottle or syringe)
3. Use at least 500mL of clean water for irrigation
4. Remove visible debris with clean tweezers (sterilize with flame first)
5. **Do NOT** use hydrogen peroxide or alcohol directly in deep wounds — it kills healthy tissue
6. Pat dry with clean cloth
7. Apply thin layer of honey (natural antibacterial) or clean petroleum jelly
8. Cover with clean bandage, change every 12-24 hours

### Signs of Infection
- Increasing redness spreading from wound
- Warmth and swelling beyond wound edges
- Pus (yellow/green discharge)
- Red streaks traveling toward heart (lymphangitis) — **CRITICAL: this is life-threatening**
- Fever above 38.5°C / 101.3°F

### Suturing (Last Resort)
- Only suture clean wounds less than 6 hours old
- **Never suture** bite wounds, puncture wounds, or visibly contaminated wounds
- Use fishing line (monofilament) and a curved sewing needle sterilized with flame
- Simple interrupted sutures, 5mm apart, 3mm from wound edge
- Remove sutures after 7 days (face) or 10-14 days (body)

## Pain Management

### Natural Analgesics

**Willow Bark Tea (contains salicin → natural aspirin)**
- Strip inner bark from white willow, weeping willow, or aspen
- Use 1-2 teaspoons of dried bark per cup of water
- Boil for 10 minutes, steep for 30 minutes
- Drink up to 3 cups per day
- **Risk**: Do not give to children under 16 (Reye's syndrome risk)
- **Risk**: Avoid if allergic to aspirin or on blood thinners

**Clove Oil (dental pain)**
- Crush whole cloves and apply directly to painful tooth
- Contains eugenol — a natural anesthetic
- Also antiseptic for oral infections

**Capsaicin (topical pain relief)**
- Crush hot peppers and mix with oil/fat
- Apply to skin over sore muscles/joints
- **Risk**: Keep away from eyes; wash hands thoroughly after

### Aspirin Synthesis (from willow bark)
1. Harvest inner bark of willow trees (2-3 large handfuls)
2. Dry bark thoroughly in sun or near fire
3. Grind to fine powder
4. Boil in water (1 part bark : 10 parts water) for 2 hours
5. Strain through cloth multiple times
6. The resulting tea contains salicin
7. **Dosage**: 1 cup of strong bark tea ≈ 1 low-dose aspirin
8. **Risk**: Overdose causes tinnitus (ringing ears), nausea, and stomach bleeding

## Antibiotics: Cultivating Penicillin

> **CRITICAL WARNING**: Improvised penicillin is extremely dangerous.
> Contamination, incorrect dosing, and allergic reactions (anaphylaxis) can
> be fatal. This information is for absolute last-resort scenarios only.

### Understanding Penicillin
- Produced by *Penicillium* mold (blue-green mold on bread/citrus)
- The mold produces penicillin as a defense against bacteria
- Wild penicillin is impure and weakly concentrated

### Cultivation Method
1. **Grow the mold**: Place bread or citrus fruit in a warm (20-25°C), dark, humid container
2. Wait for blue-green mold to appear (3-7 days)
3. **Identify**: Penicillium is blue-green, powdery, with white edges
4. **Risk**: Many molds are toxic — only blue-green *Penicillium* produces penicillin
5. Transfer mold to a sugar-water solution (sterilized)
6. Incubate at 20°C for 7 days
7. The liquid beneath the mold contains crude penicillin
8. Filter through clean cloth
9. **Risk**: This crude extract is weak, impure, and potentially contaminated
10. **Risk**: Penicillin allergy can cause fatal anaphylaxis — test with skin prick first

### Alternative Antibacterial Agents
- **Raw honey** — broad-spectrum antibacterial (apply topically)
- **Garlic** — allicin is antibacterial (crush, apply topically or eat raw)
- **Turmeric paste** — anti-inflammatory and mild antibacterial
- **Oregano oil** — contains carvacrol (topical use)
- **Sphagnum moss** — historically used as wound dressing (mildly antiseptic)

## Dental Emergencies

### Toothache
1. Rinse with warm salt water (1 tsp salt per cup)
2. Apply clove oil or crushed clove directly to tooth
3. If cavity visible, pack with temporary filling (beeswax + zinc oxide if available)
4. Ibuprofen or willow bark tea for pain

### Tooth Extraction (Absolute Last Resort)
- Only extract if: abscess is spreading, tooth is loose, severe uncontrollable pain
- Sterilize pliers/forceps with flame
- Grip tooth at root (below gum line), not crown
- Rock gently side to side, then pull with steady pressure
- Pack socket with clean gauze, bite down for 30 minutes
- Salt water rinse for 7 days after
- **Risk**: Jaw fracture, hemorrhage, infection spreading to bloodstream

## Childbirth (Emergency)

### Preparation
- Clean space, clean hands, clean cloths
- Boiled string or shoelaces for cord ties
- Clean sharp blade (sterilized) for cord cutting

### Delivery
1. Let the mother's body guide the process — do not rush
2. Support the baby's head as it emerges; do not pull
3. Check for cord around neck — if present, gently slip over head
4. After head delivers, body follows with next contractions
5. Suction mouth and nose gently with clean bulb or cloth
6. Baby should cry within 30-60 seconds
7. Keep baby warm — skin-to-skin on mother's chest
8. **Do NOT** cut the cord immediately — wait at least 1-3 minutes
9. Tie cord in two places (6 inches and 8 inches from baby)
10. Cut between ties with sterile blade
11. Placenta delivers within 5-30 minutes — do not pull on cord
12. **Risk**: Hemorrhage after delivery — massage uterus (lower abdomen) firmly

### Danger Signs Requiring Immediate Action
- **Bleeding that soaks more than 1 pad per hour** — life-threatening hemorrhage
- Baby not breathing after 1 minute — begin gentle stimulation, clear airway
- Cord prolapse (cord before baby) — mother on hands and knees, do not push
- Seizures — eclampsia, protect mother from injury
