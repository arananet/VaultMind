# Agriculture & Foraging Guide — VaultMind

> **WARNING**: Never eat any wild plant unless you are 100% certain of
> identification. Many edible plants have toxic look-alikes. When in doubt,
> do NOT eat it.

## Seed Saving

### Situation
Commercial seed supplies will not exist post-collapse. Saving seeds from
each harvest ensures food production continues indefinitely. Only
**open-pollinated** (OP) or **heirloom** varieties produce true-to-type
seeds. Hybrid (F1) seeds will not breed true.

### Easy Seeds to Save
| Crop       | Seed Type    | Method                          | Storage Life |
|------------|--------------|----------------------------------|-------------|
| Tomato     | Wet seed     | Ferment in water 3 days, rinse  | 4-10 years  |
| Beans/Peas | Dry seed     | Let pods dry on plant, shell    | 3-5 years   |
| Lettuce    | Dry seed     | Let bolt and flower, collect    | 3-5 years   |
| Squash     | Wet seed     | Scoop from mature fruit, dry    | 4-6 years   |
| Corn       | Dry seed     | Let ears dry on stalk, shell    | 2-3 years   |
| Pepper     | Wet seed     | Scoop from ripe fruit, dry      | 2-4 years   |
| Sunflower  | Dry seed     | Let heads dry, rub out seeds    | 3-5 years   |

### Seed Storage
- Dry seeds thoroughly (2-4 weeks in shade with airflow)
- Store in airtight containers with silica gel packets
- Cool, dark, dry location (ideal: 4°C / 40°F)
- Label with: variety name, date, source
- Test germination annually: place 10 seeds on wet paper towel in bag

## Soil Building

### No-Dig Composting (Fastest Soil Improvement)
1. Layer directly on ground: cardboard → 10cm compost → 5cm mulch
2. Plant into the compost layer
3. Each season, add more compost on top
4. Never turn or dig — preserves soil microbiome

### Composting
- **Browns** (carbon): dried leaves, straw, cardboard, wood chips
- **Greens** (nitrogen): food scraps, grass, manure, fresh leaves
- Ratio: 3 parts brown to 1 part green by volume
- Keep moist (like a wrung-out sponge)
- Turn every 1-2 weeks for faster decomposition
- Ready in 2-6 months (dark, crumbly, earthy smell)

### Soil Testing (Low-Tech)
- **Jar test**: Fill jar 1/3 with soil, add water, shake, let settle 24hr
  - Sand settles in 1 minute (bottom), silt in 2 hours (middle), clay in 24hr (top)
  - Ideal: 40% sand, 40% silt, 20% clay (loam)
- **pH test**: Red cabbage water indicator
  - Boil red cabbage, save purple water
  - Add soil sample to cabbage water
  - Pink/red = acidic, purple = neutral, green/yellow = alkaline
  - Most vegetables prefer pH 6.0-7.0

## Crop Rotation (4-Year Plan)

Prevents soil depletion and breaks pest/disease cycles.

| Year | Bed A        | Bed B         | Bed C         | Bed D          |
|------|-------------|---------------|---------------|----------------|
| 1    | Legumes     | Brassicas     | Root crops    | Fruiting crops |
| 2    | Brassicas   | Root crops    | Fruiting crops| Legumes        |
| 3    | Root crops  | Fruiting crops| Legumes       | Brassicas      |
| 4    | Fruiting    | Legumes       | Brassicas     | Root crops     |

- **Legumes**: beans, peas, lentils (fix nitrogen in soil)
- **Brassicas**: cabbage, broccoli, kale, turnips (heavy feeders)
- **Root crops**: carrots, potatoes, beets, onions
- **Fruiting crops**: tomatoes, peppers, squash, corn

## Animal Husbandry Basics

### Chickens (Best starter livestock)
- **Feed**: Kitchen scraps, garden waste, insects, grains, grass
- **Space**: 4 sq ft per bird (coop), 10 sq ft per bird (run)
- **Eggs**: 250-300 eggs/year per hen (first 2 years)
- **Meat**: Dual-purpose breeds: Plymouth Rock, Rhode Island Red, Australorp
- **Health**: Apple cider vinegar in water (1 tbsp/gallon) prevents parasites
- **Predator protection**: Secure coop at night; hardware cloth, not chicken wire

### Rabbits (Highest protein-to-feed ratio)
- **Feed**: Grass, hay, vegetable scraps, dandelions, clover
- **Space**: 12 sq ft per rabbit
- **Breeding**: 1 buck to 3-4 does; 30 day gestation; 6-10 kits per litter
- **Harvest**: At 10-12 weeks; ~2.5 lbs dressed meat
- **Pelts**: Tan with brain (every animal has enough brain to tan its own hide)

### Goats (Milk and land clearing)
- **Feed**: Browse (bushes, weeds, bark), hay, grain supplement
- **Milk**: 2-3 quarts/day per dairy goat (Nubian, Alpine, LaMancha)
- **Space**: 200+ sq ft per goat; they need companions (minimum 2)
- **Fencing**: 4-5 ft minimum; goats escape everything

## Wild Edible Plants (Temperate Climate)

> **CRITICAL**: Use the Universal Edibility Test before eating unknown plants.
> Never rely on a single identification feature.

### Universal Edibility Test
1. Test only one plant part at a time (leaf, root, fruit)
2. **Contact test**: Rub on inner wrist, wait 15 minutes for reaction
3. **Lip test**: Touch to lip, wait 15 minutes
4. **Tongue test**: Place on tongue, wait 15 minutes — spit out
5. **Chew test**: Chew and hold in mouth 15 minutes — spit out
6. **Swallow test**: Eat a small amount, wait 8 hours
7. If no ill effects at any stage, the plant part is likely safe
8. **Risk**: This test does NOT work for mushrooms — many toxic mushrooms
   cause delayed symptoms (24-72 hours)

### Common Safe Edibles (Northern Hemisphere)
- **Dandelion**: Entire plant edible — leaves, flowers, roots
- **Cattail**: Shoots, pollen, roots — "supermarket of the swamp"
- **Plantain** (broadleaf): Leaves edible raw or cooked; poultice for stings
- **Clover**: Flowers and leaves — high protein
- **Acorns**: Leach tannins in water (soak 3-7 days, change water daily), then grind for flour
- **Pine**: Inner bark edible (cambium layer); needle tea = vitamin C
- **Chickweed**: Small white flowers; mild salad green
- **Wood sorrel**: Heart-shaped leaves; lemony flavor; high in vitamin C

### Poisonous Plants to Avoid
- **Poison hemlock**: Looks like wild carrot — purple-spotted stems are the key difference
- **Water hemlock**: Most poisonous plant in North America — grows in wet areas
- **Death camas**: Looks like wild onion — but has NO onion smell
- **Foxglove**: Beautiful flowers; deadly cardiac glycosides
- **Castor bean**: Seeds are fatal — even 1-2 can kill an adult

## Food Preservation

### Canning (Water Bath — High Acid Foods)
1. Sterilize jars in boiling water for 10 minutes
2. Fill with hot food, leaving 1/2 inch headspace
3. Remove air bubbles with clean knife
4. Wipe rim clean; place lid and ring
5. Process in boiling water: fruits/pickles = 10-15 min, tomatoes = 35-45 min
6. Remove, let cool 12-24 hours undisturbed
7. Check seal: lid should not flex when pressed
8. **Risk**: Low-acid foods (vegetables, meat) require pressure canning to prevent botulism

### Smoking
- Build a simple smokehouse: 4 posts, walls of any material, fire pit with chimney
- Cold smoke (15-25°C) for preservation: 12-48 hours
- Hot smoke (60-80°C) for cooking: 4-8 hours
- Use hardwood only; never softwood (pine = toxic resins)

### Drying
- Slice food thin (3-6mm)
- Dry in sun on racks (2-4 days) or near fire (12-24 hours)
- Food is dry when it snaps (not bends)
- Store in airtight containers with desiccant
- Rehydrate by soaking in water for 30-60 minutes before cooking
