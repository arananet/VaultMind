# Water Systems Guide — VaultMind

> **WARNING**: Contaminated water is the #1 killer in post-disaster scenarios.
> Always purify water from unknown sources before drinking.

## Water Purification Methods

### Method Comparison

| Method                | Kills Bacteria | Kills Viruses | Removes Chemicals | Removes Sediment | Ease  |
|-----------------------|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|-------|
| Boiling (1 min)       | Yes | Yes | No  | No  | Easy  |
| Solar (SODIS, 6hr)    | Yes | Yes | No  | No  | Easy  |
| Chlorine/Bleach       | Yes | Yes | No  | No  | Easy  |
| Iodine                | Yes | Most| No  | No  | Easy  |
| Sand filter           | Most| No  | Some| Yes | Medium|
| Charcoal filter       | Some| No  | Yes | Yes | Medium|
| Ceramic filter        | Yes | No  | No  | Yes | Medium|
| Distillation          | Yes | Yes | Most| Yes | Hard  |
| Combined (filter+boil)| Yes | Yes | Some| Yes | Best  |

### Boiling
1. Bring water to a **rolling boil** for at least 1 minute
2. At altitudes above 2000m: boil for 3 minutes
3. Let cool naturally — do not add ice
4. Boiled water tastes flat — pour between containers to aerate

### Bio-Sand Filter (Long-Term Solution)
1. **Container**: Large barrel or concrete form (60-100L capacity)
2. **Layers from bottom to top**:
   - 5cm gravel (large, 10-20mm)
   - 5cm gravel (small, 5-10mm)
   - 5cm coarse sand (1-2mm)
   - 45cm fine sand (0.1-0.5mm)
   - 5cm standing water above sand (do NOT let sand dry out)
3. **Biological layer (schmutzdecke)**: Forms naturally on sand surface
   after 2-3 weeks — this is the primary pathogen removal layer
4. Pour water gently onto surface; collect from bottom drain
5. Flow rate: approximately 0.6 L/minute
6. Removes: 90-99% bacteria, 70-99% turbidity, most protozoa
7. Does NOT reliably remove viruses — combine with chlorination or boiling
8. **Maintenance**: When flow slows, scrape top 1-2cm of sand, wash, replace

### Solar Distillation
1. Dig a hole 1m wide x 0.5m deep
2. Place a collection container in the center
3. Add contaminated water, green vegetation, or wet soil around container
4. Cover hole with clear plastic sheet
5. Seal edges with soil/rocks
6. Place small stone on plastic directly above collection container
7. Sun heats water → evaporates → condenses on plastic → drips into container
8. Yield: 0.5-1.5 liters per day depending on sun and moisture
9. Removes: all pathogens, most chemicals, salt (desalination)

## Rainwater Harvesting

### Collection System
1. **Catchment**: Any clean roof surface (metal is best; avoid asphalt shingles)
2. **Gutters**: PVC pipe, bamboo, or sheet metal channels
3. **First flush diverter**: Discard first 1-2mm of rain (washes roof debris)
   - Simple: capped pipe below gutter outlet; fills first, then overflows to tank
4. **Storage**: Food-grade plastic tanks, concrete cistern, or lined pit
5. **Screen**: Mesh over tank inlet to exclude insects and debris

### Yield Calculation
- 1mm of rain on 1 m² of roof = 1 liter of water
- Example: 50 m² roof × 25mm rain event = 1,250 liters
- Annual: 50 m² × 800mm annual rainfall = 40,000 liters

### Storage
- Opaque containers (prevents algae growth)
- Sealed to prevent mosquito breeding
- Treat with 1/4 teaspoon bleach per 5 gallons for long-term storage
- Check for sediment buildup annually

## Well Drilling (Manual Methods)

### Driven Point Well (Shallow, <8 meters)
1. **Well point**: Steel tip with screen (buy pre-collapse, or fabricate)
2. Attach to galvanized pipe sections (1.25" or 1.5")
3. Drive into ground using a heavy weight on a guide (post driver)
4. Add pipe sections as you go deeper
5. Stop when you hit the water table (water rises in pipe)
6. Attach a hand pump (pitcher pump for <7.5m depth)

### Hand-Augered Well (Medium depth, <15 meters)
1. Start with a post-hole digger to 1.5m depth
2. Switch to an auger: T-handle + extension pipes + cutting blade
3. Add pipe extensions as you drill deeper
4. Remove soil by pulling auger and cleaning
5. Line with PVC well casing once desired depth is reached
6. Install well screen at bottom (slotted PVC or wrapped with mesh)
7. Pack gravel around screen (gravel pack)
8. Seal top 2m with bentonite clay or concrete (prevent surface contamination)

### Hand Pump Options
| Type          | Max Depth | Flow Rate   | Notes                        |
|---------------|-----------|-------------|------------------------------|
| Pitcher pump  | 7.5m      | 10 L/min    | Cheapest, surface suction    |
| Deep well pump| 60m+      | 5-10 L/min  | Requires more effort         |
| Rope pump     | 30m+      | 5-15 L/min  | DIY-friendly, uses rope+pistons|

## Water Testing (Field Methods)

### Turbidity
- Fill a clear container and try to read text through it
- If you can read text through 10cm of water, turbidity is acceptable
- If not: filter before chemical treatment

### Chlorine Residual
- Pool test kits (DPD tablets) work for treated water
- Target: 0.2-0.5 mg/L free chlorine after 30 minutes contact

### Biological Contamination (H2S Test)
- Presence of hydrogen sulfide (rotten egg smell) indicates bacterial activity
- Commercial H2S test strips turn black if fecal bacteria are present
- DIY: iron nails in sample water — black discoloration = sulfate-reducing bacteria

### Heavy Metals
- Not field-testable without reagents
- **Risk mitigation**: Avoid collecting water downstream of:
  - Mining operations
  - Industrial sites
  - Agricultural runoff areas
  - Landfills or dumps
