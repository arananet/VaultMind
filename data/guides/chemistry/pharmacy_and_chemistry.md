## Soap Making (Cold Process)

### Situation
Soap is the #1 defense against disease. Without soap, diarrheal illness,
wound infections, and respiratory disease spread rapidly.

### Tools
- Heat-resistant container (stainless steel or ceramic)
- Wooden or silicone stirring implement
- Mold (any container lined with parchment/cloth)
- Safety: gloves, eye protection

### Ingredients
- **Fat/Oil**: Any animal fat (tallow, lard) or vegetable oil
- **Lye (sodium hydroxide/NaOH)**: Made from hardwood ash (see below)
- **Water**: Clean, not distilled

### Making Lye from Wood Ash
1. Collect white/grey ash from hardwood fires (oak, hickory, maple)
2. Place ash in a barrel or bucket with a small drain hole at bottom
3. Pour rainwater slowly through the ash
4. Collect the liquid that drains out — this is potassium hydroxide (KOH) solution
5. **Test strength**: A chicken feather should dissolve in strong lye within 12 hours
6. If too weak, pass through fresh ash again
7. **Risk**: Lye causes severe chemical burns — always wear gloves and eye protection

### Soap Process
1. Heat fat/oil to ~38°C (100°F)
2. Slowly add lye water to fat while stirring constantly
3. Stir until mixture reaches "trace" (consistency of thin pudding)
4. Pour into molds
5. Cover and insulate for 24 hours
6. Unmold after 24-48 hours
7. Cure for 4-6 weeks (soap becomes milder as saponification completes)
8. **Risk**: Uncured soap is caustic — will burn skin

## Disinfectant Production

### Bleach Alternative (Calcium Hypochlorite)
- Pool shock (calcium hypochlorite) stores indefinitely as dry powder
- To make bleach solution: 1 teaspoon per 2 gallons of water
- For disinfecting surfaces: stronger solution (1 tbsp per gallon)
- **Risk**: Never mix with ammonia, acids, or other cleaners — produces toxic chlorine gas

### Alcohol-Based Disinfectant
- Ferment sugar water or fruit juice (see ethanol distillation in Energy guide)
- Distill to 60-70% alcohol concentration
- Effective hand sanitizer at 60%+ concentration
- Add aloe gel if available for skin moisturizing

### Pine Sol / Natural Disinfectant
1. Fill a jar with fresh pine needles
2. Cover with white vinegar
3. Seal and store in dark place for 3-4 weeks, shaking weekly
4. Strain — the resulting liquid is a mild disinfectant and cleaner
5. Pine oil has natural antibacterial and antifungal properties

## Activated Charcoal Production

### Uses
- Water filtration (removes many chemical contaminants)
- Poison ingestion treatment (absorbs toxins in stomach)
- Wound odor control
- Air filtration

### Production Method
1. Start with hardwood charcoal (not briquettes — those contain binders)
2. Crush charcoal to small pieces (pea-sized)
3. Rinse with water to remove ash
4. **Activation**: Soak in a 25% solution of calcium chloride (road salt works)
   for 24 hours — this opens pores in the carbon structure
5. Rinse thoroughly with clean water
6. Dry completely in sun or oven
7. Crush to fine powder for medicinal use, or keep granular for water filters
8. Store in airtight container

### Dosage for Poisoning
- Mix 50-100g in water, drink as slurry
- **Must be given within 1 hour of poison ingestion**
- **Risk**: Do NOT use for: acid/alkali ingestion, petroleum products, or alcohol
- Follow with a laxative (if available) to prevent constipation

## Water Treatment Chemicals

### Chlorination
- **Household bleach** (5.25% sodium hypochlorite, unscented):
  - 2 drops per liter of clear water
  - 4 drops per liter of cloudy water
  - Wait 30 minutes before drinking
- **Calcium hypochlorite** (pool shock, 65-73%):
  - Make stock solution: 1 tsp in 1 gallon water
  - Add 1 part stock to 100 parts water to treat
  - Stores for years as dry powder (liquid bleach degrades in 6-12 months)

### Iodine Treatment
- 5 drops of 2% tincture of iodine per liter
- Wait 30 minutes (60 minutes if water is cold)
- **Risk**: Not safe for pregnant women or those with thyroid conditions
- Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) neutralizes iodine taste after treatment time

### Solar Disinfection (SODIS)
- Fill clear PET plastic bottles with water
- Place in direct sunlight for 6+ hours (full day if cloudy)
- UV radiation kills bacteria and viruses
- Does NOT remove chemical contaminants
- Does NOT work with glass bottles (glass blocks UV-A)

## Preservatives and Food Safety

### Salt Curing
- Use 3-4% salt by weight for brining meat
- Dry cure: pack meat in salt, 1:4 salt-to-meat ratio
- Cure for 7-14 days in cool environment (below 5°C if possible)

### Vinegar Production
1. Start with alcohol (wine, cider, or any fermented beverage)
2. Expose to air in a wide-mouth container with cloth cover
3. *Acetobacter* bacteria will convert alcohol to acetic acid
4. Process takes 2-4 weeks
5. Vinegar is ready when pH drops below 4 (sour taste)
6. Use for preservation: pickles, sauces, meat preservation

### Smoking
- Cold smoking (below 30°C): preservation + flavor, 12-24 hours
- Hot smoking (60-80°C): cooking + preservation, 4-8 hours
- Use hardwood only: oak, hickory, maple, apple, cherry
- **Risk**: Never use pine, spruce, or treated wood — toxic resins
