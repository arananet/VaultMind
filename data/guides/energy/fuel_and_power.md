# Energy & Fuel Guide — VaultMind

> **WARNING**: Fuel production involves flammable and toxic materials.
> Work outdoors with ventilation. Keep away from open flames during
> distillation. Improper handling causes fires, explosions, and poisoning.

## Wood Gasification

### Situation
When liquid fuel is unavailable, wood gas (syngas) can power internal
combustion engines, generators, and heating systems.

### Principle
Heating wood to 700-1200°C in a low-oxygen environment produces a
combustible gas mixture: carbon monoxide (CO), hydrogen (H2), and methane
(CH4). This gas can directly fuel gasoline/petrol engines.

### Building a Simple Gasifier (FEMA-style)
#### Tools
- Steel drum or large fire extinguisher body (outer shell)
- Smaller steel container (inner reactor/hearth)
- Steel pipe fittings (1-2 inch)
- Metal grate/screen
- High-temperature gasket material
- Basic hand tools

#### Construction
1. **Inner reactor**: Steel pipe/can, ~8" diameter, 18" tall
2. Drill air inlet holes (restriction nozzles) around bottom third
3. Place metal grate at bottom for ash collection
4. **Outer shell**: Larger drum surrounds the reactor with 2-3" gap
5. This gap acts as a heat exchanger / preheater
6. **Gas outlet**: Pipe from top of reactor through a filter and cooler
7. **Filter train**: Gas passes through:
   - Cyclone separator (removes large particles)
   - Cloth/fiberglass filter (removes tar and fine particles)
   - Cooling tubes (condense remaining tar)
8. **Engine connection**: Clean, cool gas feeds into engine air intake
   via a mixing valve (gas + air ratio adjustment)

#### Operation
1. Fill reactor with dry, chunked hardwood (2-5cm pieces)
2. Light from the top and allow to burn down
3. Restrict air supply — reactor transitions to gasification
4. Gas production begins in 10-15 minutes
5. Flare the gas at outlet to check quality (steady blue flame = good)
6. Once gas is clean, connect to engine
7. **Risk**: Carbon monoxide is odorless and lethal — NEVER operate indoors
8. **Risk**: Gas contains tar — inadequate filtering destroys engines

#### Fuel Consumption
- Approximately 1 kg of dry wood produces enough gas to generate 1 kWh
- A small generator (3kW) consumes ~3 kg wood per hour

## Biodiesel Production (from vegetable oil)

### Situation
Diesel engines can run on biodiesel made from vegetable oil (new or waste
cooking oil). This is a practical fuel for tractors, generators, and
diesel vehicles.

### Ingredients
- Vegetable oil (or waste cooking oil, filtered)
- Methanol (wood alcohol) — 20% of oil volume
- Sodium hydroxide (lye) — catalyst, 3.5g per liter of oil
- **Note**: Methanol can be produced via wood distillation (see below)

### Process (Transesterification)
1. **Filter oil**: Remove food particles through cloth filter
2. **If using waste oil**: Titrate to determine extra lye needed
   (test acidity with pH strips)
3. **Make methoxide**: Dissolve NaOH in methanol (VERY carefully)
   - **Risk**: This reaction is exothermic and produces toxic fumes
   - Work outdoors, wear gloves and eye protection
4. **Heat oil** to 55°C (130°F)
5. **Add methoxide** to warm oil slowly while stirring vigorously
6. **Stir continuously** for 1 hour (or use a recirculating pump)
7. **Settle**: Pour into tall container, let sit 8-24 hours
8. **Separate**: Glycerin (dark, thick) settles to bottom
9. Drain glycerin from bottom (save — useful for soap making)
10. **Wash biodiesel**: Gently mist warm water over surface, let settle
11. Repeat wash 3-4 times until wash water runs clear
12. **Dry**: Let sit uncovered in sun for 24-48 hours

### Quality Check
- Should be clear, amber colored
- No water droplets suspended
- Burns cleanly in an oil lamp (no sputtering)
- pH between 6.5-7.5

## Ethanol Distillation

### Situation
Ethanol serves as fuel (up to E85 in flex-fuel vehicles), disinfectant
(60%+), solvent, and lamp fuel. Produced from any sugar or starch source.

### Fermentation
1. **Sugar source**: Fruit, sugar cane, honey, grain (corn, wheat, barley)
2. For grain: cook with water to convert starch to sugar (mashing)
   - Heat to 65°C for 1 hour, let cool
3. **Make wash**: Dissolve sugar in warm water (~20% sugar by weight)
4. **Add yeast**: Bread yeast works; distiller's yeast is better
5. **Seal with airlock**: Fermentation produces CO2 — needs to escape
   but air must not enter
6. **Wait**: 5-14 days at 20-30°C until bubbling stops
7. Result: ~8-15% alcohol "wash"

### Distillation
1. **Build a still**: Pot (boiler) + copper tube coil (condenser)
   - Copper pot or stainless steel pressure cooker as boiler
   - 3m+ of 10mm copper tubing coiled inside a bucket of cold water
   - Seal all joints with flour paste or food-grade silicone
2. **Heat wash** in boiler — alcohol boils at 78.4°C, water at 100°C
3. **Discard foreshots**: First 50mL per 5L of wash — contains methanol
   - **CRITICAL: Methanol causes blindness and death — always discard foreshots**
4. **Collect heads**: Next ~200mL — harsh but usable for fuel
5. **Collect hearts**: Main run at 78-82°C — purest ethanol
6. **Stop at tails**: When temperature rises above 90°C
7. First run produces ~40-60% ethanol
8. **Re-distill** for higher purity (fuel-grade needs 90%+)

### Uses by Concentration
- 40% — lamp fuel (smoky, but works)
- 60% — hand sanitizer / disinfectant
- 70% — optimal for surface disinfection
- 85%+ — engine fuel (requires carburetor adjustment)

## Solar Panel Maintenance

### Cleaning
- Wipe with soft cloth and water (no soap/chemicals)
- Clean early morning when panels are cool
- Frequency: monthly or after dust storms
- Cracked glass: seal with clear silicone to prevent moisture entry

### Common Failures
- **Bypass diode failure**: One panel in a string producing no power
  - Test with multimeter: each panel should produce rated voltage
- **Junction box corrosion**: Open box, clean contacts, reseal
- **Cable damage**: Splice with automotive wire and heat-shrink tubing
- **Charge controller failure**: Bypass with direct connection + manual monitoring
  - **Risk**: Without controller, batteries can overcharge and vent/explode

### Battery Recovery (Lead-Acid)
1. Check electrolyte level — add distilled water if low
2. Desulfation: charge at very low current (C/20) for 24-48 hours
3. Equalization charge: slight overcharge at 2.5V/cell for 2-3 hours
4. If cells are shorted: battery is beyond recovery
5. **Risk**: Charging produces hydrogen gas — ventilate area, no sparks

### Generator Repair Basics
- **No start**: Check fuel, spark plug, air filter, oil level
- **Spark plug**: Clean with wire brush, gap at 0.6-0.7mm
- **Carburetor**: Remove, soak in solvent, clear jets with fine wire
- **Low output**: Check RPM (should be 3600 for 60Hz / 3000 for 50Hz)
- **Oil substitute**: Filtered vegetable oil works short-term in emergency
  - Change every 25 hours instead of 100
  - **Risk**: Will gum up engine over time — only for emergencies

## Methanol Production (from wood)

### Principle
Destructive distillation of wood produces methanol (wood alcohol),
along with charcoal, tar, and acetic acid.

### Process
1. Fill a sealed metal retort (pipe with one open end) with dry hardwood
2. Heat externally (campfire or kiln) to 400-500°C
3. Gases and liquids exit through the open end into a condenser
4. Collect the liquid condensate (pyroligneous acid)
5. This contains: methanol, acetic acid, acetone, and water
6. Fractional distillation separates methanol (boils at 64.7°C)

### Yield
- Approximately 1-2% of wood weight becomes methanol
- 100 kg of dry wood yields ~1-2 liters of methanol

### Uses
- Biodiesel production catalyst (transesterification)
- Fuel for stoves and lamps
- Industrial solvent
- **CRITICAL: Methanol is extremely toxic — 10mL can cause blindness,
  30mL can be lethal. NEVER drink or confuse with ethanol.**
