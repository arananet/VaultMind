# Metalworking & Repair Guide — VaultMind

> **WARNING**: Metalworking involves extreme heat, sharp edges, and toxic
> fumes. Always wear eye protection, leather gloves, and work in ventilated
> areas. Never look at welding arcs without proper shade lens.

## Forge Construction

### Simple Charcoal Forge (Built from Scrap)

#### Tools Required
- Steel brake drum or fire pit
- Hair dryer or hand-crank blower (bellows)
- Steel pipe (air supply to fire pot)
- Anvil substitute: railroad rail section, large hammer head, or thick steel plate
- Hammer (2-3 lb ball peen or cross peen)
- Tongs (channel-lock pliers work initially)

#### Construction
1. **Fire pot**: Steel brake drum or hole in ground lined with fire brick
2. **Tuyere**: Steel pipe entering fire pot from side/bottom for air supply
3. **Air supply**: Hair dryer on low, or bellows made from two boards + leather
4. **Forge table**: Surround fire pot with flat surface (steel plate, packed earth)
5. **Anvil**: Secure railroad track section or heavy steel vertically in a stump
6. **Quench tank**: Bucket of water or oil nearby

#### Fuel
- **Charcoal**: Best DIY fuel — burns clean, high temperature
  - Make from hardwood in a retort (sealed burn)
- **Coal**: Bituminous (soft) coal is ideal; anthracite is harder to light
- **Wood**: Works but lower temperature, more ash

## Basic Blacksmithing Operations

### Drawing Out (Making Metal Longer/Thinner)
1. Heat steel to bright orange/yellow (900-1100°C)
2. Hammer on anvil face, rotating work piece 90° between strikes
3. Work from middle toward end
4. Reheat when color drops to dark red

### Upsetting (Making Metal Shorter/Thicker)
1. Heat the end you want to thicken
2. Strike the cold end against the anvil (like hammering a nail backward)
3. Or hammer on the hot end to compress

### Bending
1. Heat at the bend point to bright orange
2. Secure in vise or over anvil horn
3. Hammer to desired angle
4. For tight bends, use a bending fork or jig

### Punching and Drifting (Making Holes)
1. Heat metal to bright orange
2. Place punch where hole is desired, strike firmly
3. Flip work, locate the dark spot (bulge), punch from other side
4. Drive drift (tapered tool) through to size the hole

### Heat Treatment
- **Hardening**: Heat to cherry red, quench in oil or water
  - Oil = slower cool, less brittle (knives, tools)
  - Water = faster cool, harder but more brittle
- **Tempering**: After hardening, reheat to straw/blue color
  - Straw (220°C) = knives, cutting tools
  - Blue (300°C) = springs, punches (more flexible)
- **Annealing**: Heat to cherry red, let cool slowly in ash (softens for machining)

## Tool Making

### Knife from a File
1. Anneal the file: heat to cherry red, bury in ash overnight
2. Shape the blade profile with angle grinder or by forging
3. Grind bevels (15-20° per side for general use)
4. Harden: heat to cherry red (non-magnetic point), quench in oil
5. Temper: bake at 200°C for 1 hour (or heat until straw color appears)
6. Handle: wood scales + epoxy + pins, or wrap in cord/leather

### Axe Head Repair
1. If handle hole is loose: soak head in water (wood swells), or add wedge
2. If edge is chipped: grind past the chip, re-profile, re-temper
3. If cracked: a cracked axe head is dangerous — retire it

### Nail/Fastener Making
1. Heat rod stock to bright orange
2. Hammer to point on anvil
3. Cut to length with hardy (chisel in anvil)
4. Form head with heading tool (plate with hole) or by upsetting

## Brazing and Soldering (Without Electricity)

### Soldering (Low Temperature Joining, <450°C)
- **Heat source**: Propane torch, or copper soldering iron heated in fire
- **Flux**: Rosin (pine resin) for electronics; zinc chloride for plumbing
- **Solder**: Lead-tin alloy (60/40) — melt on joint, not on iron
- **Use**: Electrical connections, tin work, plumbing patches

### Brazing (Higher Strength, 450-900°C)
- **Heat source**: Oxy-fuel torch, or forge heat
- **Filler**: Brass rod (copper-zinc alloy)
- **Flux**: Borax powder (dissolve in water, apply as paste)
- **Process**:
  1. Clean joint surfaces thoroughly
  2. Apply flux paste
  3. Heat joint (not filler) until flux turns glassy
  4. Touch filler rod to joint — it should flow into the gap by capillary action
  5. Let cool slowly
- **Use**: Steel-to-steel joints, tool repair, pipe repair

## Vehicle Repair Basics

### Engine Won't Start — Diagnostic Flow
1. **Battery**: Lights work? If dim/none → charge/replace battery
2. **Starter**: Clicking but no crank → bad starter or connections
3. **Fuel**: Crank but no start → check fuel flow (prime pump, check filter)
4. **Spark**: Pull spark plug, ground against block, crank — spark visible?
5. **Air**: Check air filter — clogged filters starve engines
6. **Timing**: Did timing belt/chain break? (Check if engine spins freely)

### Tire Repair (Without a Shop)
1. Locate puncture: inflate, submerge in water, watch for bubbles
2. Remove object with pliers
3. Ream the hole with a tire reamer (T-handle rasp tool)
4. Thread plug through insertion tool
5. Push plug through hole until 1cm protrudes inside
6. Pull tool out — plug stays
7. Trim exterior flush
8. Reinflate and test

### Fuel System (Emergency Measures)
- **Fuel filter clogged**: Bypass temporarily with inline mesh screen
- **Fuel line leak**: Repair with rubber hose + hose clamps
- **Water in fuel**: Drain from lowest point in fuel system; add isopropyl alcohol
- **Diesel won't start in cold**: Preheat intake air, or add small amount of gasoline (<5%)
  - **Risk**: Too much gasoline in diesel damages injection pump

### Coolant System
- **Emergency coolant**: Plain water works temporarily
- **Radiator leak**: Egg whites in coolant (seriously — they coagulate and plug small leaks)
- **Hose repair**: Wrap with rubber + safety wire, or duct tape as temporary fix
- **Overheating**: Check: coolant level, thermostat stuck, water pump belt, fan operation
