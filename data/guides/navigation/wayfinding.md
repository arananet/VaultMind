# Navigation Guide — VaultMind

> **NOTE**: GPS satellites will eventually fail without ground station
> maintenance. Learn these skills before you need them.

## Compass and Map Reading

### Compass Basics
- **Magnetic north ≠ True north**: Difference is "declination"
- Declination varies by location (check local maps for value)
- To convert magnetic to true: add east declination, subtract west
- Hold compass level; let needle settle completely before reading

### Taking a Bearing
1. Point direction-of-travel arrow at target
2. Rotate bezel until needle aligns with orienting arrow (red in the shed)
3. Read bearing at index line
4. To follow bearing: hold compass flat, rotate body until needle is in the shed

### Map to Terrain (Triangulation)
1. Identify two or more landmarks visible on both map and terrain
2. Take compass bearing to each landmark
3. Draw back-bearings on map (bearing + 180°)
4. Your position is where lines intersect
5. Three bearings = triangle of error (you're inside it)

### Pace Counting
- Count every time your LEFT foot hits the ground for 100 meters
- Average: 62-68 paces per 100m on flat ground
- Adjust for terrain: add 10% uphill, subtract 5% downhill
- Use beads or knots on a string to track hundreds of meters

## Celestial Navigation

### Finding North (Northern Hemisphere)

**By Polaris (North Star):**
1. Find the Big Dipper (Ursa Major)
2. Follow the two "pointer stars" (front edge of the cup) upward
3. They point to Polaris — the last star in the Little Dipper's handle
4. Polaris is within 1° of true north
5. Altitude of Polaris above horizon = your latitude

**By the Sun:**
1. **Shadow stick method**:
   - Place a 1m stick vertically in the ground
   - Mark the tip of shadow; wait 15-30 minutes; mark again
   - Line between marks runs east-west (first mark = west)
   - Perpendicular to this line = north-south
2. **Watch method** (analog watch):
   - Point hour hand at the sun
   - South is halfway between hour hand and 12 o'clock
   - (In Southern Hemisphere: point 12 at sun, north is halfway to hour hand)

### Finding South (Southern Hemisphere)

**By the Southern Cross:**
1. Find the Southern Cross (Crux) — four bright stars in a cross shape
2. Extend the long axis 4.5 times its length
3. Drop a line straight down to the horizon
4. That point is approximately due south

### Estimating Time
- The sun moves 15° per hour across the sky
- Fist at arm's length ≈ 10° of sky
- Sun's position at solar noon = due south (N. Hemisphere) / due north (S. Hemisphere)

## Dead Reckoning

### Principle
Track your position by recording direction and distance from a known point.

### Method
1. Start from a known position (marked on map)
2. Record bearing of travel
3. Count paces (see Pace Counting above)
4. At each direction change, record new bearing and reset pace count
5. Plot each leg on the map: bearing + distance
6. Accumulated error grows over time — recalibrate with landmarks

### Reducing Error
- Cross-reference with terrain features (ridgelines, rivers, road crossings)
- Use "handrails" — follow linear features (rivers, ridges, trails)
- Use "backstops" — features you cannot walk past (large river, cliff, road)
- "Aim off" — deliberately navigate to one side of your target,
  then follow a linear feature to it (so you know which way to turn)

## Terrain Association

### Reading Terrain Without a Map
- **Water flows downhill** — follow streams to find larger water and civilization
- **Ridgelines** — travel on ridges for visibility and dryer footing
- **Valleys** — usually have water, trails, and settlements
- **Vegetation changes** — darker green = more water; sparse = drier/higher

### Estimating Distance
| Method                    | Accuracy | Notes                           |
|---------------------------|----------|---------------------------------|
| Pace counting             | ±5%      | Best on known terrain            |
| Thumb method (known size) | ±15%     | Object size / thumb width × 100  |
| Sound timing              | ±20%     | Thunder: count seconds / 3 = km  |
| Terrain features          | Varies   | Ridge-to-ridge on topo map       |

### Slope Estimation
- **Clinometer from protractor**: Hang a weight from protractor center,
  sight along flat edge, read angle where string crosses
- **1:4 or steeper** (>14°) — difficult footing
- **1:2 or steeper** (>27°) — hands needed
- **1:1 or steeper** (>45°) — rope/climbing required

## Natural Navigation Indicators

### In Northern Hemisphere
- **Moss**: Tends to grow on north side of trees (more shade/moisture)
  - NOT reliable as sole indicator — check multiple trees
- **Spider webs**: Often face south (warmth attracts insects)
- **Ant hills**: Often on south side of trees (warmth)
- **Snow melt**: Melts faster on south-facing slopes
- **Tree growth**: Branches often fuller on south side (more sunlight)
- **Satellite dishes**: Usually point south (toward geostationary belt)

### Wind Patterns
- Prevailing winds come from west in mid-latitudes (30-60°)
- Trees lean away from prevailing wind
- Sand dunes' slip face points downwind
- Cloud movement indicates wind direction at altitude
